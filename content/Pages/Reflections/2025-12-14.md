## An Update on [[2025-02-07|My Faith Crisis]]

I came across this Wikipedia article on the [acceptance of evolution by religious groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acceptance_of_evolution_by_religious_groups) and at the time of writing, the article has a chart showing the percentage of different religious groups who agreed that [[Evolution|evolution]] is the best explanation for the origin of human life on Earth as of 2007. 

![[Religion-Evolution-Acceptance.png]]

What was a bit surprising to me was how low [[Mormonism]] was on this chart. As I've pointed out earlier in my [[2025-02-07#Looking to the Church|Looking to the Church]] section of my faith crisis write up, some church leaders were strongly against the idea of human evolution. But I also found this [excerpt from the Encyclopedia of Mormonism](https://contentdm.lib.byu.edu/digital/collection/EoM/id/3666) interesting. It quotes First Presidency statements on the origin of man from 1909 and 1925, and an internal 1931 First Presidency memo.

![[Encyclopedia-Mormonism-Evolution.png|316]]

The excerpt reads as fairly neutral, but it leaves some things out. The 1909 statement originally included this line: "It is held by some that Adam was not the first man upon this earth, and that the original human being was a development from lower orders of the animal creation. These, however, are the theories of men." The 1925 version dropped that line, and the encyclopedia only quotes what the two have in common. Yet the Church reprinted the full 1909 statement, line included, in the February 2002 Ensign.

The 1931 memo is the part that stood out to me. It was written to settle a debate among church leaders, and its conclusion was to "leave geology, biology, archaeology, and anthropology... to scientific research" while still affirming that "Adam is the primal parent of our race." In other words, the First Presidency decided the Church shouldn't take sides on the science. So the official record itself is mixed, and that's part of the problem.

What confuses me is why so many church leaders from the 1950s to today, as seen in [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MiebbpNqpDs) that I also previously mentioned in [[2025-02-07#Looking to the Church|My Faith Crisis]], went to the pulpit and came out fully against evolution anyway. The First Presidency had already told them to leave it to science. And I'm not going to go into the flawed men argument. These are supposed leaders of [[God]]'s church speaking on behalf of the church and its leadership. Their statements, whether true or not, should hold equal weight.

Going back to the chart above, the second striking thing to me is how willing the Jewish community is to agree with evolution. This is a religious community that relies on the Hebrew Bible (what Christians call the [[Old Testament]]) along with centuries of rabbinic commentary, yet they are more accepting of evolution than Christians. From what I can find, most Jews view the story of creation allegorically or philosophically rather than as a scientific fact. I'm not sure why modern [[Christianity]] and LDS views skew towards upholding the [[Book of Genesis]] account of life's origin as scientific, but to me it's clearly a problem and will continue to be a problem Christianity as a whole is going to have to reconcile with as science encroaches on explanations that used to be left up to [[Bible]] interpretation. [[Adam and Eve]] are portrayed as real beings in the LDS mythos with [[Michael the Archangel]] being a pre-embodied Adam and him being essential in the creation of [[Earth]]. Again, we get into the issue of how much of the story is "real" and how much of it is "allegorical".
